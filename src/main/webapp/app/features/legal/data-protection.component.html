Data Processing Addendum

This Data Processing Addendum (“DPA”) forms part of the Terms of Service between {{ legal.entity }} (“{{ legal.product }}”, the “Processor”) and the Customer (the “Controller”). It applies whenever {{ legal.product }} processes personal data in the Customer’s workspace, and sets out the terms required by Article 28 of the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (“GDPR”) and equivalent laws (together, “Data Protection Law”). If this DPA and the Terms conflict on data protection, this DPA prevails.

1. Roles and scope

The Customer is the controller of the personal data in its workspace (“Customer Personal Data”) and {{ legal.product }} is its processor. The Customer is responsible for having a lawful basis for the processing, for the instructions it gives, and for informing its employees and other data subjects about it. The subject matter, nature, purpose and duration of the processing and the categories of data and data subjects are described in Annex 1.

2. Processor obligations

{{ legal.product }} will:

  1. Instructions — process Customer Personal Data only on the Customer’s documented instructions, which are the Terms, this DPA and the Customer’s configuration and use of the service, including for international transfers, unless the law requires otherwise (in which case it will tell the Customer first, unless the law forbids that). It will tell the Customer if it believes an instruction breaches Data Protection Law.
  2. Confidentiality — ensure that everyone it authorises to process Customer Personal Data is bound by confidentiality and has access only as far as their role requires.
  3. Security — implement and maintain the technical and organisational measures in Annex 2 (GDPR art. 32).
  4. Sub-processors — engage other processors only as set out in section 3.
  5. Data subject requests — taking into account the nature of the processing, help the Customer by appropriate technical and organisational measures to respond to requests to exercise data subjects’ rights; the service lets the Customer view, correct and delete employee records itself, and download documents and payroll exports. If a data subject contacts {{ legal.product }} directly, it will pass the request on to the Customer and not respond itself, except to direct them to the Customer.
  6. Assistance — help the Customer meet its obligations on security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation with supervisory authorities (GDPR arts. 32–36), taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to it.
  7. Deletion or return — at the end of the service, let the Customer export its data and then delete Customer Personal Data as described in section 6, unless the law requires it to be kept.
  8. Audits — make available the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA, and allow and contribute to audits, as set out in section 7.

3. Sub-processors

The Customer gives general authorisation for {{ legal.product }} to engage sub-processors. The current list is in Annex 3. {{ legal.product }} will give at least {{ legal.subprocessorNoticeDays }} days’ notice (by email to the workspace owner or by updating this page with notice in the app) before a new sub-processor starts processing Customer Personal Data. The Customer may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that period; the parties will then discuss it in good faith and, if they cannot resolve it, the Customer may terminate the affected service and receive a refund of prepaid fees for the remaining period. {{ legal.product }} imposes data protection obligations on each sub-processor that are no less protective than this DPA, and remains liable to the Customer for their performance.

4. International transfers

Customer Personal Data is hosted in {{ legal.hostingRegion }}. {{ legal.product }} will transfer it outside the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland only where an adequacy decision applies or appropriate safeguards are in place — such as the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by the European Commission (Decision 2021/914, Module 2 controller-to-processor, or Module 3 for onward transfers to sub-processors), the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, or the recipient’s certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework — together with any supplementary measures required. Where the Standard Contractual Clauses apply between the parties, they are incorporated into this DPA by reference, with Annexes 1–3 completing their appendices, and they prevail if they conflict with it.

5. Personal data breaches

{{ legal.product }} will notify the Customer without undue delay, and in any event within {{ legal.breachNoticeHours }} hours, after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting Customer Personal Data. The notice will describe, as far as then known, the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, its likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address it, with further information provided as it becomes available. {{ legal.product }} will take reasonable steps to contain and remedy the breach and help the Customer meet its own notification obligations. Notifying a breach is not an admission of fault.

6. Duration, return and deletion

This DPA lasts as long as {{ legal.product }} processes Customer Personal Data. The Customer can export its data from the service before the subscription ends, and for {{ legal.deletionDays }} days afterwards on request (the workspace is reopened for it). After that, {{ legal.product }} deletes the workspace’s database and stored files from its live systems, and backups are overwritten within a further {{ legal.backupDays }} days, except where the law requires retention — in which case the data stays protected by this DPA and is used for no other purpose. On request, {{ legal.product }} will confirm deletion in writing.

Within the service, the Customer also controls retention: it can delete employee records and documents, and set retention periods for records such as performance reviews, after which they are purged.

7. Audits

On written request, no more than once a year (or after a personal data breach, or at a supervisory authority’s request), {{ legal.product }} will answer the Customer’s reasonable security questionnaires and provide documentation of its measures. If that is not enough to show compliance, the Customer may carry out an audit itself or through an independent auditor bound by confidentiality, with at least 30 days’ notice, during business hours and in a way that does not disrupt the service or compromise other customers’ data. Each party bears its own costs.

8. Liability and general

Each party’s liability under this DPA is subject to the limits in the Terms, except where Data Protection Law does not allow liability to data subjects to be limited. This DPA is governed by the same law as the Terms, unless the Standard Contractual Clauses require otherwise. Contact for data protection matters: {{ legal.privacyEmail }} · {{ legal.dpo }}.

Annex 1 — Description of the processing

Subject matter and nature
Hosting, storing, organising, calculating, displaying, transmitting, exporting and deleting HR and payroll data as part of the {{ legal.product }} service.
Purpose
Providing the service to the Customer: employee records and organisation structure, onboarding and offboarding, leave and absence, timesheets, documents, performance management, compensation, payroll calculation and payslips, approvals workflows, reporting and support.
Duration
The term of the Customer’s subscription, plus the export and deletion period in section 6.
Data subjects
The Customer’s current, former and prospective employees, contractors and other workers; their emergency contacts where the Customer records them; and the Customer’s users of the service.
Categories of personal data
  • Identity and contact: name, photo, date of birth, gender, marital status, nationality, addresses, email, phone.
  • Government identifiers: national ID, passport, social security and tax identification numbers.
  • Employment: job, position, department, manager, contracts, start and end dates, work schedule, education, training and licences.
  • Pay and financial: salary and compensation, pay components, deductions, tax, payslips, bank account (IBAN) details.
  • Time and absence: leave requests and balances, timesheets.
  • Performance: goals, reviews, feedback, ratings, improvement plans.
  • Documents uploaded by the Customer or its users, and account and activity data (sign-ins, devices, audit logs).
Special categories
Only as the Customer chooses to record them — for example an employee’s medical profile (blood type, allergies, emergency medical notes), health-insurance details, or reasons for sick leave. The Customer is responsible for having a lawful condition under GDPR art. 9 for this data and for limiting it to what is necessary; it is protected by the measures in Annex 2.
Frequency
Continuous, for the duration of the service.

Annex 2 — Technical and organisational measures

  • Tenant isolation — each workspace has its own separate database; requests are bound to a single workspace and cannot read another’s data.
  • Encryption in transit — connections between browsers and the service, and between the service and its databases, use TLS.
  • Encryption at rest — [describe disk / database / file-storage encryption provided by the hosting provider].
  • Access control — role- and permission-based access inside each workspace, enforced on the server for every request; sensitive areas such as payroll and compensation require specific permissions; managers see only their reporting lines.
  • Authentication — passwords are stored only as salted one-way hashes (bcrypt); sign-in sessions use signed tokens; users can review and sign out their devices; password resets use single-use, expiring links.
  • Logging and monitoring — security-relevant actions are recorded in audit logs; [describe availability and error monitoring].
  • Internal access — {{ legal.product }} staff access customer data only where needed to provide support or operate the service, on a least-privilege basis and under confidentiality obligations.
  • Resilience and backups — [describe backup frequency, retention and restore testing].
  • Secure development — automated tests, dependency updates and a restrictive content-security policy in the web application.
  • Data minimisation and retention — nothing is stored for a sign-up until its email is confirmed; customers can delete records and set retention periods; workspace data is deleted at the end of the service as in section 6.
  • Incident response — a documented process to contain, investigate, remedy and notify personal data breaches (section 5).
  • Sub-processor management — sub-processors are chosen for their security and bound by written data protection terms.

{{ legal.product }} may update these measures over time, provided the overall level of protection is not reduced. Report a security issue to {{ legal.securityEmail }}.
